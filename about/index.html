About CTRI

Building outside the market.

The textbooks that exist in Nigeria are not the problem. The students who cannot access them are. CTRI exists to close that gap.

What CTRI Is

CTRI — the Curriculum and Textbook Resource Initiative — is a programme to write, review, and publish free, contextualised textbooks for Nigerian senior secondary school students. The textbooks cover subjects examined at WAEC and are designed specifically for the SS1–SS3 curriculum.

CTRI is an initiative of Care for Knowledge, a Nigerian nonprofit focused on improving access to quality educational resources.

The Problem We Are Solving

Three barriers stand between Nigerian students and the learning resources they need. The cost barrier — quality textbooks exist, but many students cannot afford them. The context barrier — free resources exist, but most were written outside Nigeria, for different students. The register barrier — even good, contextualised textbooks are often written in formal academic language that is itself a barrier to comprehension.

These barriers compound each other, and they fall hardest on students in hard-to-reach communities — rural areas, underserved states — precisely the students for whom the SS1–SS3 years matter most.

What Makes CTRI Different
Written for Nigerian students, by people who know Nigeria

CTRI textbooks are written specifically for the Nigerian SS1–SS3 curriculum, by contributors who understand the Nigerian context. Examples, analogies, and illustrations draw on realities that Nigerian students recognise. This is original content developed from within the context it serves — not a translation or adaptation.

Conversational, not academic

CTRI textbooks are written in a register that sounds like a knowledgeable teacher explaining something to a student — not a formal academic text. The language of a textbook is part of how it works. A student who has to work through formal register before they can access content has a harder time than one whose textbook speaks to them directly.

University students as contributors

CTRI does not rely exclusively on subject matter experts. University-level contributors — people close in age and experience to the secondary school students the textbooks serve — bring contextual sensitivity and remember what it felt like not to understand something.

Two Phases

Phase 1 — Write and Publish Online. Develop textbooks for SS1–SS3 subjects through a structured writing, editing, and review process. Publish as open educational resources — free to access, download, and print. Subject to iterative improvement through student feedback. Goal: 10 textbooks by 2030, with a long-term aim to cover all WAEC subjects.

Phase 2 — Print and Distribute Offline. Because CFK owns the copyright to its textbooks, it can print and distribute without licensing fees — making physical distribution economically viable. This phase will reach students in communities where internet access is limited.

Where We Are Now

CTRI is in its earliest phase. The theory of change is sound. The model is well-designed. What CTRI needs to realise its potential is time, contributors, and funding — to organise writing sprints, source reference materials, and build the infrastructure to eventually get these textbooks into the hands of students who cannot access them online.

The Core Commitment

Free, contextualised, conversational, owned by the community that produces it. That is a different model. It is the right model for this problem.
